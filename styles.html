    Style library

    Interior design styles explained

    Definitions, signature materials and the mistakes to avoid for ten popular styles. Not sure which is yours? Take the quiz.

    Japandi

    A blend of Japanese minimalism and Scandinavian comfort. Low furniture, pale and mid-tone woods, linen, paper and stone. Every object earns its place.

    Signature materials: Light oak, ash, linen, washi paper, ceramic, matte black accents

    Avoid: Clutter, glossy finishes, loud patterns

    Find a Japandi designer

    Modern Minimal

    Clean planes, flush storage and a restrained palette. The architecture is the decoration, and negative space is a feature.

    Signature materials: Lacquer, plaster, glass, steel, wool

    Avoid: Ornate trims, visible cables, too many accessories

    Find a Modern Minimal designer

    Mid-Century Modern

    Born in the 1940s–60s: tapered legs, organic curves, teak and walnut, and confident colour like mustard, teal and burnt orange.

    Signature materials: Walnut, teak, moulded plywood, brass, bouclé

    Avoid: Heavy, skirted furniture

    Find a Mid-Century Modern designer

    Warm Industrial

    Loft character — brick, concrete, black steel and reclaimed wood — warmed with leather, rugs and plants.

    Signature materials: Brick, steel, reclaimed timber, leather, Edison bulbs

    Avoid: Overly cold, all-grey schemes

    Find a Warm Industrial designer

    Modern Farmhouse

    Rustic comfort with modern restraint: white walls, shiplap, oak, matte-black fixtures and practical, family-friendly pieces.

    Signature materials: White oak, shiplap, linen slipcovers, stoneware

    Avoid: Kitschy signage overload

    Find a Modern Farmhouse designer

    Bohemian

    Collected, layered and personal: global textiles, rattan, vintage finds and abundant greenery.

    Signature materials: Rattan, jute, kilims, macramé, terracotta

    Avoid: Matching furniture sets

    Find a Bohemian designer

    Coastal

    Light, breezy and relaxed with washed whites, soft blues and sandy textures — no nautical clichés required.

    Signature materials: Linen, rattan, whitewashed wood, seagrass

    Avoid: Anchors and themed décor everywhere

    Find a Coastal designer

    Art Deco Glam

    1920s-inspired drama: geometric patterns, brass, lacquer, velvet and jewel tones.

    Signature materials: Brass, marble, velvet, lacquer, fluted glass

    Avoid: Timid scale — Deco needs confidence

    Find a Art Deco Glam designer

    Scandinavian

    Bright, functional and cosy (hygge). Pale woods, white walls, soft textiles and simple forms.

    Signature materials: Birch, pine, wool, sheepskin

    Avoid: Dark, heavy furniture

    Find a Scandinavian designer

    Traditional / Classic

    Symmetry, moulding, rich woods and timeless patterns like damask and stripes, updated with lighter palettes.

    Signature materials: Mahogany, silk, damask, crystal

    Avoid: Mixing too many eras at once

    Find a Traditional / Classic designer
