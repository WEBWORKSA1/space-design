    Lighting Design Basics: Layers, Lumens and Colour Temperature

    By the Space.Design editorial team · 7 min read · Updated 2026-11-16

    Learn ambient, task and accent lighting, how many lumens each room needs and how to choose bulbs that flatter your space.

    Lighting changes how big, warm and expensive a room feels more than almost any other upgrade — and it's one of the cheapest to get right.

    The three layers

    • Ambient — the general fill: ceiling fixtures, recessed lights, cove lighting.
    • Task — focused light for reading, cooking and grooming: desk lamps, under-cabinet strips, vanity sconces.
    • Accent — highlights art, shelves and texture: picture lights, uplights, LED tape.

    A well-lit room uses all three and puts them on separate switches or dimmers.

    How many lumens?

    RoomFoot-candlesExample: 150 sq ft room
    Living / bedroom10–201,500–3,000 lm
    Dining30–404,500–6,000 lm
    Kitchen30–404,500–6,000 lm
    Home office30–504,500–7,500 lm
    Bathroom70–8010,500–12,000 lm

    Multiply room square footage by foot-candles — or let our lumens calculator do it.

    Colour temperature (Kelvin)

    • 2700K: warm, cosy — living rooms and bedrooms.
    • 3000K: warm-neutral — kitchens and bathrooms that still feel inviting.
    • 3500–4000K: crisp — offices, garages, laundry.

    Keep one colour temperature per room so surfaces don't look patchy.

    CRI matters

    Colour Rendering Index measures how accurately a light shows colours. Choose CRI 90+ in kitchens, bathrooms and anywhere you choose paint or clothes.

    Placement rules of thumb

    • Pendants over an island: 30–36" above the counter.
    • Chandelier over a dining table: about 30–34" above the tabletop, roughly half to two-thirds the table width.
    • Vanity lights: at face height on both sides of the mirror beats a single bar overhead.
    • Recessed lights: start about 2–3 ft from walls to avoid harsh scalloping.
