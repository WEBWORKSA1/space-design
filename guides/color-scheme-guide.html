    How to Choose a Colour Scheme for Your Home (Without Regret)

    By the Space.Design editorial team · 7 min read · Updated 2026-11-16

    Use the 60-30-10 rule, undertones and light direction to choose wall, fabric and accent colours that work together.

    Paint is cheap; repainting isn't. These principles help you pick colours that look right in your light, with your floors and furniture.

    Start with what you can't change

    Flooring, kitchen counters, fireplace stone and large furniture all have undertones — warm (yellow, red, orange) or cool (blue, green, violet). Pick wall colours whose undertones agree with them.

    The 60-30-10 rule

    • 60% dominant colour: walls, large rugs.
    • 30% secondary: upholstery, curtains, an accent wall.
    • 10% accent: cushions, art, accessories.

    Our palette generator builds a 60-30-10 scheme from any base colour.

    Read your light

    • North-facing rooms (northern hemisphere) get cool, indirect light — warmer whites and earthy tones balance them.
    • South-facing rooms are bright and warm — cooler tones stay fresh.
    • East is warm in the morning, cool later; west the reverse.

    Classic harmony models

    • Monochromatic: shades of one hue — calm and sophisticated.
    • Analogous: neighbours on the colour wheel — relaxed and natural.
    • Complementary: opposites — energetic; use one as the small accent.
    • Triadic: three evenly spaced hues — playful; mute two of them.

    Test before you commit

    1. Paint large swatches (at least 2 × 2 ft) or use peel-and-stick samples.
    2. Put them on two different walls.
    3. Look morning, afternoon and night for two days.
    4. Check them next to your flooring and fabrics.

    Finish guide

    • Matte / flat: ceilings, low-traffic walls — hides flaws.
    • Eggshell: living spaces — slight sheen, wipeable.
    • Satin: kitchens, bathrooms, kids' rooms.
    • Semi-gloss: trim, doors, cabinets.
