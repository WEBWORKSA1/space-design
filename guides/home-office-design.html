    Home Office Design: Ergonomics, Light and Layout for Real Productivity

    By the Space.Design editorial team · 8 min read · Updated 2026-11-16

    Design a home office that protects your back, eyes and focus — desk heights, monitor placement, lighting, acoustics and storage.

    A productive home office is mostly about three things: posture, light and fewer distractions. Style comes after.

    Ergonomics by the numbers

    ElementGuideline
    Desk height (seated)Elbows at about 90°, usually 28–30" (71–76 cm) for average adults; adjustable is best
    MonitorAbout an arm's length away, top line at or slightly below eye level
    ChairFeet flat, thighs roughly parallel to the floor, lumbar support
    Desk depthAt least 24" (61 cm); 30" for large monitors
    Chair clearance behind desk36" (91 cm) to push back and stand comfortably

    Where to put the desk

    Place the desk perpendicular to a window so daylight comes from the side — facing the window causes glare and squinting, and putting your back to it puts glare on the screen. If you take video calls, face the light source and keep a tidy, simple background behind you.

    Light it in layers

    • Ambient: overhead or indirect light so the room isn't a cave.
    • Task: an adjustable desk lamp placed opposite your writing hand.
    • Colour temperature: 3500–4000K keeps you alert; switch to warmer light in the evening.

    Use our lumens calculator — offices typically need 30–50 foot-candles of light.

    Sound and focus

    • Soft surfaces — rugs, curtains, upholstered chairs, bookcases — absorb echo.
    • A door sweep and a solid-core door help more than any gadget.
    • Keep only today's work on the desk; everything else gets a closed home.

    Small-space solutions

    • Wall-mounted fold-down desks for hallways and bedrooms.
    • A closet conversion ("cloffice") with shelves above and a 24"-deep worktop.
    • A console desk behind a sofa doubles as a room divider.
