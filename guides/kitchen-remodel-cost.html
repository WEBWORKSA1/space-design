    Kitchen Remodel Cost Guide: What You'll Really Spend and Where

    By the Space.Design editorial team · 10 min read · Updated 2026-11-16

    A practical breakdown of kitchen renovation budgets — cost tiers, where the money goes, resale value and ways to save.

    The kitchen is the most expensive room per square foot to renovate, and the easiest to overspend on. Use this guide to set a realistic budget before you talk to contractors.

    Every figure here is a broad planning range in USD. Labour rates, codes and material prices vary widely by region — always get at least three written quotes. Try our renovation budget estimator for a room-specific range.

    Three budget tiers

    ScopeWhat it usually includesTypical range
    RefreshPaint, cabinet refacing or painting, new hardware, lighting, backsplash, one or two appliances$10,000–$30,000
    Mid-range remodelNew semi-custom cabinets, quartz or solid-surface counters, mid-range appliances, new flooring, same layout$35,000–$80,000
    Major / upscaleLayout changes, moving plumbing and gas, custom cabinetry, premium appliances, structural work$90,000+

    Where the money goes

    A common rule of thumb for a full remodel:

    • Cabinets & hardware: ~30%
    • Labour & installation: ~20%
    • Appliances & ventilation: ~15%
    • Countertops: ~10%
    • Flooring, lighting, plumbing fixtures, backsplash: ~15%
    • Design, permits, contingency: ~10%

    The decisions that move the number most

    1. Keeping the layout. Moving a sink, range or gas line triggers plumbing, electrical and often permit and inspection costs. Keeping the footprint is the single biggest saver.
    2. Cabinet grade. Stock, semi-custom and custom cabinetry can differ by a factor of three or more for the same run.
    3. Counter material. Laminate, quartz, granite, natural marble and sintered stone span a wide price band per square foot.
    4. Appliance package. Pro-style ranges and panel-ready refrigerators can cost more than an entire refresh.

    Resale value

    Industry cost-versus-value studies have consistently found that modest, mid-range kitchen updates recover a much larger share of their cost at resale than upscale major remodels. If you plan to sell within a few years, spend on cabinets fronts, lighting, counters and appliances buyers see, not hidden luxury.

    Ten ways to save without looking cheap

    • Refinish or reface sound cabinet boxes instead of replacing them.
    • Mix open shelving with fewer upper cabinets.
    • Use a premium material only on the island, a cheaper one on the perimeter.
    • Buy floor-model or previous-year appliances.
    • Choose standard cabinet sizes.
    • Do demolition and painting yourself if you're able.
    • Order everything before demolition to avoid idle-trade days.
    • Use large-format tile — fewer grout lines, faster install.
    • Upgrade lighting: under-cabinet LEDs change a kitchen dramatically for little cost.
    • Keep a 10–20% contingency so surprises don't force downgrades later.

    Timeline

    Plan 2–4 weeks for design and ordering, 4–12 weeks of construction for a mid-range remodel, and longer for custom cabinets or permits.
