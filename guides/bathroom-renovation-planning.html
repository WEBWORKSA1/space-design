    Bathroom Renovation Planning: Layout, Clearances and Budget

    By the Space.Design editorial team · 9 min read · Updated 2026-11-16

    Plan a bathroom remodel the right way — fixture clearances, ventilation, waterproofing, materials and realistic costs.

    Bathrooms pack plumbing, electrical, ventilation and waterproofing into a tiny footprint. Get the plan right and everything else is easier.

    Fixture clearances

    ItemRecommendedCommon code minimum*
    Toilet centreline to side wall/fixture18" (46 cm)15" (38 cm)
    Clear space in front of toilet30" (76 cm)21" (53 cm)
    In front of vanity30"+21"
    Shower interior36 × 36" (91 cm)30 × 30"
    Vanity height32–36" (81–91 cm)—

    *Codes vary by country, state and municipality. Confirm local requirements before building.

    Keep the wet wall

    Moving a toilet drain is one of the most expensive bathroom changes. Arranging fixtures along the existing plumbing wall keeps cost and risk down.

    Waterproofing and ventilation are non-negotiable

    • Use a proper waterproof membrane behind tile in showers — tile and grout alone are not waterproof.
    • Size the exhaust fan to the room and duct it outside, never into the attic.
    • Slope shower floors toward the drain; consider linear drains for curbless showers.

    Materials that last

    • Porcelain tile: dense, low-absorption, great for floors and showers.
    • Quartz vanity tops: low-maintenance, stain-resistant.
    • Solid-brass valves and cartridges: worth the premium inside walls.
    • Large-format tiles: fewer grout lines to clean.

    Budget ranges

    A cosmetic refresh (vanity, toilet, lighting, paint, mirror) is often in the low five figures or less; a full gut with new tile, glass and fixtures typically costs considerably more, especially in high-cost cities. Run your numbers through our budget estimator, then collect three quotes.

    Accessibility tip: Install blocking in the walls for future grab bars while they're open — it costs little now and a lot later.
