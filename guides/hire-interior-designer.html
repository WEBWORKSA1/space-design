    How to Hire an Interior Designer: Fees, Questions and Red Flags

    By the Space.Design editorial team · 9 min read · Updated 2026-11-16

    Understand designer fee structures, what to ask before signing, and how to tell a great fit from an expensive mistake.

    A good designer saves money by preventing mistakes, unlocking trade pricing and managing trades. A poor fit costs time and money. Here's how to choose well.

    Designer, decorator, or e-designer?

    • Interior designer: plans space, finishes, lighting and often coordinates construction drawings. In some regions the title is regulated and requires certification.
    • Decorator: focuses on furnishings, colour, textiles and styling — no structural work.
    • E-designer / online design service: remote concept boards, floor plans and shopping lists, usually flat-fee per room. Great value for a single room or tight budgets.

    How designers charge

    ModelHow it worksBest for
    HourlyCommonly $100–$300+/hour depending on market and experienceConsultations, small projects
    Flat fee per roomFixed price per room or package; online services often a few hundred dollars per roomClear, defined scope
    Percentage of projectOften 10–30% of total project costFull renovations
    Cost-plusDesigner buys at trade price and adds a markupFurnishing-heavy projects

    Ask exactly what's included: number of concepts, revisions, site visits, drawings, procurement and installation day.

    Twelve questions to ask before signing

    1. Can I see completed projects similar to mine in size and budget?
    2. Who will I work with day-to-day?
    3. How do you charge, and what triggers extra fees?
    4. Do you pass trade discounts on to clients?
    5. How many revision rounds are included?
    6. Will you produce scaled floor plans and elevations?
    7. Do you manage contractors, or recommend them?
    8. What's a realistic timeline from concept to install?
    9. How do you handle back-orders and damaged deliveries?
    10. Are you insured and, where relevant, licensed?
    11. Can I speak with two recent clients?
    12. What happens if we part ways mid-project?

    Red flags

    • No written contract or a vague scope.
    • Pressure to pay large deposits on furniture before plans are approved.
    • Portfolio that's all renderings with no completed work.
    • Unwillingness to discuss budget openly.
